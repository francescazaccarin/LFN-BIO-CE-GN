\section{Abstract}
Gene interaction networks allow to examine the structural organisation of genetic systems and identify a number of genes with unique topological roles based on graphs. In this report, the genetic interaction corpus of BIO-CE-GN for \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans}, which we developed by building an undirected interaction network while using network-science methods we studied in class, will be explored.

We obtain node-level centrality dimensions (degree centrality and betweenness centrality and PageRank) to represent various understandings of gene importance. Pearson correlations are also employed to test the relationship between these measures, and to confirm that they offer redundant or complementary ranking.

Moreover, as a qualitative approach, community detection is used to investigate mesoscopic network structure, and to infer that clustered groups of genes are candidate functional modules. The extent of agreement between degree centrality and PageRank reflects the fact that local connectivity is heavily responsible for global significance within this network, as well as the fact that betweenness centrality captures additional and complementary structural information that signals potential connector genes.

In the wider context of the course, therefore, in this paper network based analysis is presented in a manner that provides an interpretable structural view of gene interaction data.

\section{Introduction}

Gene interaction networks provide a graph-based representation of genetic relationships, where genes are modelled as nodes and interactions as edges. Analysing such networks with graph-theoretic tools can reveal structural patterns that are informative about candidate regulators, hub genes, and higher-level organisational structure.

In this project, we investigate the BIO-CE-GN dataset of genetic interactions in \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans} by constructing a gene interaction network and studying it through node-level centrality measures and correlation analysis. Specifically, we compute degree centrality, betweenness centrality, and PageRank to capture complementary notions of node importance, and we examine the correlation between these measures to understand how different centrality-based rankings agree or differ.

Beyond node-level properties, we also discuss community structure as a qualitative framework to interpret densely connected groups of genes as candidate functional modules. Overall, the goal is to provide an interpretable structural characterisation of the BIO-CE-GN interaction network using techniques introduced during the course, and to outline how these network-level findings can motivate biological follow-up analyses.

\section{Dataset and Network Construction}

The analysis is based on the BIO-CE-GN dataset of genetic interactions in \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans}. The data was visualized and shown in the form of a network of genes where nodes correspond to genes and edges correspond to interactions between genes. In this graph representation, it makes it easy to use the usual network analysis methods to characterize the structural behaviour of the interaction system.

Self-loops were dropped before preprocessing to avoid artificially inflating node centrality scores. The network was modelled as a simple undirected graph, which guarantees a uniform treatment of the centrality measures and supports comparison across metrics. This modeling decision adheres to the project instructions and is consistent with the network representations introduced in class.

The resulting network has 2,220 nodes and 53,683 edges (average degree = 48.36; density = 0.0218). The graph is comprised of three connected components which suggests that most genes are linked together well, but few nodes make up a small set of isolated subgraphs. The number of units in the network also influences its characteristics. A connectivity pattern indicates a predominantly integrated interaction network.

These fundamental network statistics, thus, illustrate the interaction structure globally, which will serve as the foundation for the centrality and correlation analysis discussed later in the page.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Centrality Analysis}

In biological interaction networks, various structural roles could be associated with differing biological hypotheses. Hub-like (high degree or PageRank) genes may be representative of those broadly interacting genes, and bridge-like (high betweenness) genes may indicate those genes that organize the interactions across modules. Hence, joint interpretation of centrality scores is more informative than considering a single measure alone.

To characterize the structural roles of individual genes in the BIO-CE-GN interaction network, we calculated three node-level centrality measures: degree centrality, betweenness centrality, and PageRank. There is a parallel set of nodes with values in different axes of centrality in the topology as reported in the above metrics.

Degree centrality indicates local connectivity by counting the number of direct interactions, or “hubs,” of a gene in the network. Genes that are central in a big way can be regarded as hubs and potentially involved in a wide variety of genetic interactions.

Betweenness centrality represents the fraction of shortest pathways in the network and shows genes that serve as a bridge between nodes. These nodes may also act as intermediaries that connect areas of interaction structure which are not very connected and help to facilitate connectivity in the network.

PageRank adds some global weight to the concept of importance by considering not only the number of connections between a gene but also the relevance of its neighbouring genes with a random walk. Consequently, genes with high PageRank are not necessarily the most important but rather the embedded genes in influential layers of the network.

All centrality scores were computed on the preprocessed undirected simple graph from the BIO-CE-GN edge list (self-loops removed). Degree centrality measured local connectivity, PageRank gave a global ranking depending on neighbourhood influence, and betweenness centrality reflected potential connector nodes that connect different areas of the network. Employing multiple measures lessens the bias of a single concept of importance, while giving a more reliable structural typology of candidate genes.


\subsection{Correlation between Centrality Measures}

Correlation analysis is valuable in assessing whether different metrics yield the same or alternative ranking.

The wide correspondence between degree and PageRank implies that, in this network, global influence is dominated by local connectivity. In contrast, the weaker betweenness is indicative that bridge-like nodes can rarely be deduced from degree and PageRank. In reality, this encourages prioritisation of the candidate species that were continuously central across degree and PageRank, and separate screening for high betweenness genes as possible inter-module connectors.

To investigate the association among various concepts of node importance, we calculated the Pearson correlation between the centrality measures described above. The correlation matrix obtained is displayed using a heatmap visualization to display general agreement and differences between degree centrality, betweenness centrality and PageRank.

The relationship of degree centrality to PageRank shows a high positive correlation, showing that genes with numerous direct interactions are also more likely to have global network effects. In comparison, betweenness centrality has weaker correlations with the two other measures, indicating it captures complementary structural information by identifying genes with a mediating or connector function in different regions within the network.

The heatmap visually summarises the pairwise Pearson correlations between the three centrality measures. Warmer colours indicate stronger positive correlations, while cooler colours correspond to weaker associations. The diagonal elements reflect the trivial self-correlation of each measure, whereas the off-diagonal entries highlight the degree of agreement between different notions of node importance.

\subsection{Community Detection and Assessment of Functional Relevance}

In addition to node-level centrality measures, the project also considers the mesoscopic organisation of the network through community detection. Communities are defined as groups of nodes that are more densely connected to each other than to the rest of the graph, and they provide insight into higher-level structural patterns that cannot be captured by individual node metrics alone.

Following the project guidelines, the analysis is restricted to network-based methods introduced during the course. In this context, community detection is performed using modularity-based approaches, which aim to identify partitions of the network that maximise intra-community connectivity while minimising inter-community links. These methods are commonly adopted in network science and are consistent with the techniques discussed in class.

The detected communities are interpreted as structural modules emerging from the topology of the gene interaction network. In biological networks, such modules are often considered candidate functional units, as genes that are densely interconnected may participate in related biological processes or pathways.

The correspondence between communities and known biological pathways is assessed at a qualitative and exploratory level. Rather than performing external biological enrichment analyses, which are beyond the scope of this course, the evaluation focuses on network-based properties such as the internal connectivity of communities and the presence of highly central genes within clusters. This approach provides an initial indication of the potential functional relevance of the detected communities while remaining fully consistent with the methodological constraints of the course.


\section{Discussion}

The centrality analysis results give a single structural structure for BIO-CE-GN gene interaction network. The strong agreement found between degree centrality and PageRank confirms the universality of relationships where genes with many direct interactions have globally-influential properties within the network. This indicates that global importance by PageRank in this dataset is mostly local to connectivity and highly connected hub genes tend to occupy the structurally dominant locations in the interaction network.

In contrast betweenness centrality tends to correlate less strongly with both degree centrality and PageRank, implying it is able to extract complementary structural information. In contrast, nodes with high betweenness do not necessarily possess many direct links; only large fractions of shortest paths lie between regions of the network themselves. Genetically, these genes may serve coordination and/or mediating purposes through coordinating interactions among otherwise loosely interconnected modules. This contrast supports a concurrent application of combined assessment of multiple measures of centrality: if the whole focus is on a single concept of importance one might potentially miss genes structurally important for network integration, rather than merely local connectivity.

The qualitative community analysis contributes to an enriched interpretation of the network because the focus is now on mesoscopic organisation of the network, in contrast to that of individual genes. These modularity-based means of mapping communities expose genes in a cluster closer together, which is more closely linked to one another than to the rest of the network. In biology-associated interactions, the dense networks of clusters is commonly regarded as candidate functional modules representing coordinated or related biological mechanisms that are usually considered as part of the biological interaction networks.

Studying the internal relationships between communities and highly central genes occurring within clusters can give an interpretable and network-based view on potential functional significance. Importantly, the community analysis is purposely limited to a qualitative evaluation based on network structure only. While external biological enrichment analyses, which fall outside the scope of this course, were deemed beyond the scope of this course, the interpretation takes into account topological properties derived from the interaction network. In fact, it provides methodological continuity with the methods discussed in the course but still offers substantial insights into structural phenomena at higher levels.

\paragraph{Limitations}

A number of drawbacks, however, must be understood. First, the BIO-CE-GN is formulated as an undirected and unweighted network, e.g., the data structure of which simplifies the interactions and directionality or a strength of interaction may not be considered in the data. Second, centrality measures are inherently sensitive to network topology and preprocessing choices, and high centrality does not always imply biological causality. Finally lack of external biological validation limits the extent of interpretable functional inferences made purely by network structure.

\paragraph{Future work}
An extension of this work in the future might include external biological data for cross-referencing existing biological information relevant to validate and contextualize the network-based results. For example, high-scoring genes determined in centrality analyses can be mapped to known pathways or functional annotations, and detected communities compared to curated biological modules. Methodologically, additional robustness assessments may investigate alternative modeling designs, including weighted networks or separate analyses for the largest connected component. Such extensions would bridge the gap between structural network investigation and biological interpretation.


\section{Conclusion}
We analyzed the BIO-CE-GN gene interaction dataset for \textit{Caenorhabditis elegans}, using a network-based approach, in this paper. Using degree centrality, betweenness centrality and PageRank to analyse the structural roles of individual genes in the interaction mechanism, we modeled genes as nodes and interactions as edges.

Results indicate a significant agreement between degree centrality and PageRank, while betweenness centrality provides complementary insight by detecting potential connector genes. Moreover, a qualitative community perspective was employed to analyze mesoscopic structure and propose highly connected gene clusters as promising functional modules.

Even though the analysis focused mainly on network-based techniques discussed during the course, joint node and community-level perspectives create a consistent structural characterization of the network. In the general perspective, this work showcases the use of graph-theoretical techniques to investigate and uncover gene interaction networks with a view to generating interpretable hypotheses relevant to biological investigations.
